Protein-fiber sensors for robotic touch

Touch tells your hand how hard to grip, when something slips and what you're holding. We're developing sensors to give robot hands the same sense.

Scroll ↓

Pressure

Your fingertips feel how hard you're pressing, and your grip adjusts without you thinking about it.

A robot needs it toPick up a ripe tomato without crushing it.

Vibration

A spider feels its prey through vibrations that travel along the silk of its web.

A robot needs it toNotice the moment it touches something, and the moment it starts to slip.

Shape

A rat sweeps its whiskers back and forth to feel the shape and texture of things in the dark.

A robot needs it toFeel objects its cameras can't see, such as the ones hidden behind its own fingers.

Protein fibers

The spider's web and the rat's whiskers are both protein fibers that carry touch to the animal.

We're developingTouch sensors made from protein fibers, for robot hands and grippers. We'll share more here as the work develops.
