Glass Noodle: spinning milk proteins into hollow-core optical fibers

We're growing hollow glass fibers from casein, the protein that makes milk white. Casein is an inexpensive by-product of dairy, so it could make these fibers from an abundant starting material. The project is funded by ARIA's Universal Fabricators programme.

Read The project and full team on ARIA ↗

Why hollow-core fibers

Hollow-core optical fibers guide light through air instead of solid glass, so signals travel faster and lose less. Today they are drawn from glass at around 2,000 °C, which takes a lot of energy and is hard to scale.

How we're making them

The process has two stages, so the fiber's shape and the glass growing on it can each be tuned on their own. Each step below is active research.

  1. Engineer the proteinModify casein to behave like the silica-forming proteins found in nature, so it controls where and how glass grows.
  2. Spin the fiber hollowBuild on known ways of spinning casein fibers, with in-situ monitoring to control the shape.
  3. Grow the glassSilica forms on the protein template.
  4. Remove the proteinWhat's left is a hollow silica fiber.
